# Voltage

> **See also:** [[quick-context/electric-current]] | [[quick-context/parallel-vs-series-voltage]]

**Definition:** The electrical "pressure" or potential difference between two points, measured in volts. Voltage is the force that pushes [[quick-context/electric-current|current]] through a circuit—it represents how much energy each unit of charge carries.

```
WATER TOWER ANALOGY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

     ┌─────────┐
     │  WATER  │  ← Height = VOLTAGE
     │  TANK   │    (potential energy
     └────┬────┘     per unit water)
          │
          │ pressure pushes
          │ water down
          ▼
     ───────────  ← Ground level (0V)

Higher tank = more pressure = more voltage
But voltage alone does nothing without a path
for current to flow (like a closed valve)
```

**Key insight:** Voltage doesn't "flow" or get "used up"—it's a *difference* between two points. A 9V battery always has 9V between its terminals whether powering a tiny LED or sitting in a drawer.
