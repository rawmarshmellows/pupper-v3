# Case: Where Electric Charge Comes From

> **Components:** [[quick-context/atoms-molecules-polymers-basics]] | [[quick-context/electric-current]] | [[quick-context/voltage]]
> **Micro-context:** [[micro-context/coulomb-history]] | [[micro-context/current-electrons-per-second]]

> **In brief:** Electric charge is a fundamental property of subatomic particles—protons are positive, electrons are negative. This isn't "caused" by anything else; it's intrinsic. Objects become charged when they have unequal numbers of protons and electrons. The [[quick-context/voltage|electric field]] between charges is what pushes [[quick-context/electric-current|current]] through circuits.

## The Situation

You know that electric fields are created by positive and negative charges. But where do these charges come from? What makes something "positive" or "negative" in the first place?

## The Pieces

**Atoms:** Everything is made of atoms, which contain three types of particles: protons (positive), neutrons (neutral), and electrons (negative). See [[quick-context/atoms-molecules-polymers-basics]] for the full hierarchy.

**Charge as a fundamental property:** Positive and negative charge are intrinsic properties of protons and electrons—like mass, they just *are*. We can't explain them in terms of something simpler.

**Imbalance creates net charge:** A neutral object has equal protons and electrons. Remove or add electrons, and you get a charged object.

## Step by Step: From Atoms to Electric Fields

### Step 1: Inside the Atom

Every atom has a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by orbiting electrons.

```
THE STRUCTURE OF AN ATOM
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

                    ELECTRON CLOUD
                 ╭─────────────────╮
                ╱                   ╲
               ╱    ─e⁻              ╲
              │                       │
              │      ┌───────┐        │
              │      │ ⊕ ⊕   │ ←──────┼─── NUCLEUS
              │      │  ⊙    │        │    ⊕ = proton (+1)
              │      │ ⊕ ⊙   │        │    ⊙ = neutron (0)
              │      └───────┘        │
              │             ─e⁻       │
               ╲     ─e⁻             ╱      e⁻ = electron (-1)
                ╲                   ╱
                 ╰─────────────────╯

    CARBON ATOM EXAMPLE:
    • 6 protons   → total charge: +6
    • 6 neutrons  → total charge:  0
    • 6 electrons → total charge: -6
    ─────────────────────────────────
      NET CHARGE:                  0  (neutral atom)
```

### Step 2: The Fundamental Charges

Here's the key insight: **charge is a fundamental property, like mass**.

```
THE THREE SUBATOMIC PARTICLES

    Particle    │  Charge           │  Mass              │ Location
    ════════════╪═══════════════════╪════════════════════╪══════════════
    Proton      │  +1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C    │  1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg    │ In nucleus
    Neutron     │   0               │  1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg    │ In nucleus
    Electron    │  -1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C    │  9.1×10⁻³¹ kg     │ Orbits nucleus
                                        ↑
                                   ~1836× lighter
                                   than proton!

    WHY IS CHARGE "FUNDAMENTAL"?
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    You can ask: "Why does a proton have mass?"
    Answer: Because it's made of quarks that have mass.

    You can ask: "Why does a proton have positive charge?"
    Answer: Because it's made of quarks (2 up, 1 down) whose
            charges add to +1.

    But then: "Why do quarks have charge?"
    Answer: ...that's where explanations stop.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  Charge is one of the fundamental properties of matter,      │
```

### Step 3: Why "Positive" and "Negative"?

The names are arbitrary—Benjamin Franklin named them in the 1750s before anyone knew what was actually moving.

```
THE NAMING OF CHARGES
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

    1752: Benjamin Franklin experiments with static electricity
          ───────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Franklin rubbed different materials together and observed
    two types of "electrical fluid":

        Glass rubbed with silk    → one type (he called it "+")
        Rubber rubbed with fur    → other type (he called it "-")

    He arbitrarily chose which was which. 50/50 chance.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  BAD LUCK: Franklin guessed wrong about which way           │
    │  "electrical fluid" flowed. He assumed + moved to -.        │
    │                                                              │
    │  REALITY: Electrons (negative) are what actually move       │
    │  in most circuits. Protons stay locked in atomic nuclei.    │
    │                                                              │
    │  We're stuck with "conventional current" (+ to -)           │
    │  even though electron flow is (- to +).                     │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    1897: J.J. Thomson discovers the electron
          ───────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Now we know: the "negative" charge carriers are electrons,
    and they're what move in wires. But the naming stuck.
```

### Step 4: How Objects Become Charged

Atoms are normally neutral (equal protons and electrons). Charge imbalance happens when electrons move.

```
CREATING CHARGED OBJECTS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

    NEUTRAL OBJECTS: equal protons and electrons
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────

        ┌────────────────┐     ┌────────────────┐
        │  ⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕       │     │  ⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕       │
        │  e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻ │     │  e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻ │
        │     OBJECT A   │     │     OBJECT B   │
        │   (neutral)    │     │   (neutral)    │
        └────────────────┘     └────────────────┘


    RUBBING TRANSFERS ELECTRONS (e.g., balloon on hair):
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────

        ┌────────────────┐     ┌────────────────┐
        │  ⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕       │     │  ⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕⊕       │
        │  e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻ ════╪═══▶│  e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻e⁻ │
        │     OBJECT A   │     │  e⁻e⁻          │
        │                │     │     OBJECT B   │
        └────────────────┘     └────────────────┘
             │                        │
             ▼                        ▼
        6 protons              6 protons
        4 electrons            8 electrons
        ────────────           ────────────
        Net: +2                Net: -2
        (POSITIVE)             (NEGATIVE)


    WHY DO ELECTRONS MOVE BUT PROTONS DON'T?
    ─────────────────────────────────────────

    • Electrons orbit loosely around atoms
    • Some can be "rubbed off" by friction
    • Protons are locked in the nucleus by the strong nuclear force
    • You'd need to split the atom to move a proton

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  KEY INSIGHT: Charge isn't created or destroyed.            │
    │  It's just redistributed. If A gains +2, B gains -2.        │
    │  Total charge in universe: always zero.                     │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Step 5: How Charges Create Electric Fields

Once you have charge separation, an electric field exists in the space between them.

```
FROM CHARGES TO ELECTRIC FIELD
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

    SINGLE POSITIVE CHARGE → field radiates outward:
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

                    ↑
                ↗       ↖
              ↗     ⊕     ↖
                ↘       ↙
                    ↓

        Field points away from positive charge
        (direction a positive "test charge" would be pushed)


    SINGLE NEGATIVE CHARGE → field points inward:
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────

                    ↓
                ↙       ↘
              ↙     ⊖     ↘
                ↖       ↗
                    ↑

        Field points toward negative charge


    TWO OPPOSITE CHARGES → field lines connect them:
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

              ⊕ ─────────────────────────────→ ⊖
              ⊕ ─────────────────────────────→ ⊖
              ⊕ ─────────────────────────────→ ⊖
              ⊕ ─────────────────────────────→ ⊖
              ⊕ ─────────────────────────────→ ⊖

        This is the electric field in a battery or capacitor!
        This field is what pushes electrons through wires.
        See [[quick-context/voltage]] for how this drives current.
```

## Why Each Piece Matters

- **Protons and electrons:** The actual carriers of charge—without them, no electricity
- **Charge conservation:** Explains why static electricity always creates +/- pairs
- **Electron mobility:** Why electrons (not protons) are what flows in circuits
- **Electric field:** The mechanism by which charges exert force at a distance
