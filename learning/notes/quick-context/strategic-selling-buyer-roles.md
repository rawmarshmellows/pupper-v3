# Strategic Selling Buyer Roles

> **Related:** [[learning/notes/quick-context/miller-heiman-strategic-selling-lamp]] | [[learning/notes/quick-context/meddpicc-qualification-framework]] | [[learning/notes/quick-context/sales-methodology-layering]] | [[learning/notes/quick-context/mcdonald-kam-model]] | [[learning/notes/quick-context/challenger-sale-methodology]]

> **TL;DR:** The four buyer roles—Economic Buyer, User Buyer, Technical Buyer, and Coach—are a Miller Heiman framework for mapping decision-makers in complex B2B sales, where each role has different criteria, access points, and influence over the deal outcome.

## The Core Problem

Sales reps often sell to whoever will take a meeting, then discover they've been talking to someone without authority to buy. Complex deals involve multiple stakeholders with different priorities—the person who uses your product daily cares about different things than the person writing the check. Without mapping these roles, reps misjudge deal health, miss key influencers, and get blindsided by objections from stakeholders they never engaged.

## 5 Essential Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Economic Buyer** | The single person with discretionary authority to approve the purchase—can say "yes" when everyone else says "no," and controls the budget |
| **User Buyer** | The person(s) who will work with your solution day-to-day and judges success based on job impact and personal convenience |
| **Technical Buyer** | The gatekeeper who screens against specifications, compliance, or technical requirements—can veto but cannot approve alone |
| **Coach** | An internal advocate who provides intelligence about the organization's decision process, politics, and how to navigate to the Economic Buyer |
| **Buyer Mode** | The psychological stance of each buyer: Growth (seeking improvement), Trouble (reacting to pain), Even Keel (status quo), or Overconfident (dismissive) |

**Role Characteristics:**

```
┌──────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Role         │ Key Characteristics                                       │
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ECONOMIC     │ • Question: "What's the return on this investment?"       │
│ BUYER        │ • Concern: Business impact, risk, ROI                     │
│              │ • Access: Difficult—executives are busy                   │
│              │ • Mistake: Never meeting them before proposal             │
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ USER         │ • Question: "How will this affect my daily work?"         │
│ BUYER        │ • Concern: Ease of use, training, job impact              │
│              │ • Access: Easy—they're motivated to solve their problem   │
│              │ • Mistake: Assuming they have authority they don't        │
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ TECHNICAL    │ • Question: "Does this meet our specifications?"          │
│ BUYER        │ • Concern: Compliance, security, integration, standards   │
│              │ • Access: Medium—gatekeepers but willing to evaluate      │
│              │ • Mistake: Treating their "no" as final (escalate)        │
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ COACH        │ • Question: "How can I help you succeed here?"            │
│              │ • Concern: Your success (for various reasons)             │
│              │ • Access: Usually easy—they seek you out                  │
│              │ • Mistake: Confusing Coach for Champion (see MEDDPICC)    │
└──────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Buyer Modes (Psychological Stance):**

Each buyer exists in one of four modes affecting their receptivity:

```
┌─────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Mode        │ Meaning & Strategy                                         │
├─────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ GROWTH      │ Actively seeking improvement, open to change               │
│             │ Strategy: Lead with vision and future possibilities        │
├─────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ TROUBLE     │ Reacting to a problem, urgent need                         │
│             │ Strategy: Show quick relief, address pain directly         │
├─────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ EVEN KEEL   │ Satisfied with status quo, no perceived need               │
│             │ Strategy: Create dissatisfaction, show hidden risks        │
├─────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ OVER-       │ Dismissive, believes current approach is sufficient        │
│ CONFIDENT   │ Strategy: Very hard—may need crisis or external pressure   │
└─────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

<summary><strong>The Key Tension</strong> — Coverage vs. efficiency</summary>

The framework creates pressure to engage all buyers, but time is limited:

| Full Coverage | Efficient Coverage |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Meet all four roles before proposing | Focus on Economic + key influencer |
| Multiple discovery calls | Consolidated qualification |
| Lower risk of surprise objections | Faster deal velocity |
| Longer sales cycles | Risk of late-stage blockers |

**The practitioner debate:**

- **"Always map all buyers"**: Any unmapped buyer can kill the deal. A veto from a Technical Buyer you never met is worse than a longer cycle.
- **"Focus on the money"**: Economic Buyer is the only one who can say yes. Get to them fast, let them manage their organization.

**The pragmatic answer:** Scale coverage to deal size. $10K deal? Meet Economic Buyer and one User Buyer. $100K+ deal? Full stakeholder map and engagement plan for all four roles.

</details>

<summary><strong>Concrete Example</strong> — Mapping buyers in a real deal</summary>

**Scenario:** Selling a $75K security monitoring platform to a 300-person fintech.

```
STAKEHOLDER MAP
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

ECONOMIC BUYER: CFO (Maria Chen)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Authority: Final approval on tech spend >$50K
• Mode: EVEN KEEL — not feeling urgency
• Status: NOT MET YET ⚠️
• Win Theme: Cost avoidance (prevent breach-related losses)
• Challenge: She doesn't know we exist; all activity is below her level
• Next Step: Coach to arrange introduction after VP Eng buy-in

USER BUYER: DevOps Team Lead (James Wu) + Security Analyst (Rachel Kim)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Authority: Heavy influence on product selection; no budget authority
• Mode: TROUBLE — 3AM pages weekly, overwhelmed by false positives
• Status: Engaged, enthusiastic
• Win Theme: Sleep. Alert fatigue relief. Career protection.
• Challenge: May not articulate pain in CFO language
• Next Step: Help them quantify downtime cost for exec presentation

TECHNICAL BUYER: VP Engineering (David Park) + Security Lead (Priya Patel)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• David Mode: GROWTH — wants to build best-in-class security
• Priya Mode: EVEN KEEL — current tools "work fine" in her view
• Authority: Can veto on technical/compliance grounds
• Status: David engaged; Priya skeptical
• Win Theme: SOC2 compliance, integration with existing stack
• Challenge: Priya's "even keel" makes her a potential blocker
• Next Step: Technical deep-dive with Priya to address concerns

COACH: IT Manager (Alex Rivera)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Relationship: Former customer at previous company, strong advocate
• Power: Limited—reports to James, no exec access
• Intel Provided:
  - Budget cycle in Q4, need proposal before Sept
  - CFO trusts David's recommendations
  - Priya was burned by last vendor, hence skepticism
• Status: Active, providing ongoing intelligence
• Risk: Coach not Champion—provides info but can't drive action
• Next Step: Help Alex become Champion or find someone with more power

COVERAGE ASSESSMENT:
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
✓ User Buyers: Strong coverage, enthusiastic
? Technical Buyers: David yes, Priya needs more work
✗ Economic Buyer: No coverage—single biggest gap
✓ Coach: Active but low-power

PRIORITY ACTIONS:
1. Address Priya's concerns (Technical Buyer gap)
2. Use David to access Maria (Economic Buyer gap)
3. Arm James with ROI data for internal selling
```

**The one thing most outsiders get wrong about this is...** they think the roles are job titles. A "VP of Engineering" could be Economic Buyer at one company and Technical Buyer at another—it depends on who actually controls the budget. Similarly, a CEO might be nominally Economic Buyer but in practice rubber-stamps their CTO's decisions. The roles describe *decision-making functions*, not org chart positions.

<summary><strong>Test Your Understanding</strong> — 5 progressive questions</summary>

**Q1:** What's the key difference between Economic Buyer and Technical Buyer authority?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Economic Buyer can approve—they control the budget and can say "yes" when everyone else says "no." Technical Buyer can only veto—they screen for compliance and specifications but cannot approve the purchase alone. See: How It Works.
</details>

**Q2:** Your deal has strong User Buyer support but you've never met the Economic Buyer. What's the risk?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
User Buyers can't approve budget. Without Economic Buyer engagement, your deal can stall at proposal stage when it escalates for approval and the EB has no context, relationship, or urgency. Common failure: months of work invalidated by someone you never met asking "why do we need this?"
</details>

**Q3:** A Coach says "I think the CFO would approve this." Is this enough Economic Buyer coverage?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
No. Coach *opinion* isn't the same as Economic Buyer *engagement*. You need to validate the Coach's assessment, ideally by meeting the EB yourself. Coaches have limited visibility; their predictions may be wrong, especially about executives above them.
</details>

**Q4:** How do buyer modes change your approach to the same role?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
A User Buyer in TROUBLE mode is receptive—lead with quick pain relief. A User Buyer in EVEN KEEL mode needs disruption—use [[learning/notes/quick-context/challenger-sale-methodology|Challenger]] insights to create dissatisfaction with status quo. Same role, opposite strategies. Mode determines receptivity; role determines criteria.
</details>

**Q5:** Your Technical Buyer (VP Security) is in OVERCONFIDENT mode about their current solution. How do you proceed?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
OVERCONFIDENT is the hardest mode—they don't see the problem. Options: (1) Go around them to Economic Buyer with business case (risky—they may feel bypassed), (2) Wait for a crisis that shakes their confidence, (3) Use industry data/peer examples to create doubt, (4) Find a different sponsor who can influence them. Sometimes the right answer is disqualify—a blocker in OVERCONFIDENT mode can kill deals regardless of other support.
</details>
