# Coulomb (Unit History)

> **[[learning/notes/quick-context/tensile-strength-materials|]]See also:** [[quick-context/electric-current]] | [[micro-context/current-electrons-per-second]]

**[[learning/notes/quick-context/capacitance|Definition]]:** [[learning/notes/quick-context/capacitance|The]] coulomb ([[learning/notes/micro-context/cathode|C]]) is the SI unit of electric [[learning/notes/quick-context/cations-and-reduction|charge]], named after French physicist Charles-Augustin de Coulomb (1736–1806). Originally defined by electrostatic force experiments, it's now defined as the charge transported by [[learning/notes/quick-context/meddpicc-qualification-framework|1]] ampere in 1 second.

## How It Works

- Coulomb measured electrostatic force between charged objects using [[learning/notes/quick-context/anions-and-oxidation|a]] torsion balance, establishing $F \propto q_1 q_2 / [[learning/notes/quick-context/cations-and-reduction|r]]^[[learning/notes/quick-context/meddpicc-qualification-framework|2]]$.
- The unit was later redefined in terms of [[learning/notes/quick-context/capacitance|current]]: 1 coulomb = the charge carried by 1 ampere in 1 second.
- The 2019 SI revision fixed the elementary charge exactly, making 1 coulomb = exactly [[learning/notes/quick-context/tensor|6.]]241509074 × 10¹⁸ [[learning/notes/quick-context/anions-and-oxidation|electron]] charges.

```
TIMELINE:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1785   Coulomb's torsion balance experiments
       → quantifies electrostatic force (F ∝ q₁q₂/r²)

1881   International definition adopted
       → 1 C = charge depositing 0.001118g silver

1948   Redefined via ampere
       → 1 C = 1 A × 1 s (current-based)

2019   SI revision
       → 1 C = exactly 6.241509074×10¹⁸ electrons
```

**Key insight:** The coulomb was defined before [[learning/notes/quick-context/anions-and-oxidation|electrons]] were discovered (1897)—the unit came from macroscopic force measurements, [[learning/notes/micro-context/push-pull-vs-open-drain|and]] [[learning/notes/quick-context/comparator-specification|only]] later was mapped to electron [[learning/notes/quick-context/switches-to-registers-storing-data|count]].
