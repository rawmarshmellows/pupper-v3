# Oxidation

> **See also:** 

**Definition:** The loss of electrons by an atom, ion, or molecule—always paired with reduction (electron gain) somewhere else, forming a redox reaction. Despite the name, oxygen need not be involved.

## How It Works

- An atom or ion releases one or more electrons, increasing its oxidation state (becoming more positive).
- The released electrons must be accepted by another species (the oxidizing agent), which undergoes reduction — the two half-reactions are always coupled.
- The driving force is the difference in electron affinity: electrons flow from species that hold them weakly to species that attract them strongly.

```
Oxidation = losing electrons

   BEFORE           AFTER
   ──────           ─────
     Fe      →     Fe²⁺  +  2e⁻
  (neutral)      (positive)  (released)

  Atom loses electrons → becomes more positive
  OIL: Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons)

  Examples:
    Fe   → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻   (rust)
    Zn   → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻   (battery anode)
   2Cl⁻  → Cl₂  + 2e⁻   (electrolysis)
```

**Key insight:** Oxidation doesn't require oxygen—it just means losing electrons. Rusting iron, a battery discharging, and chlorine forming during electrolysis are all oxidation because electrons leave, regardless of whether oxygen participates.
